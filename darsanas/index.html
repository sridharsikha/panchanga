Ṣaṇmata and Ṣaḍdarśana

How the Śaṅkara tradition organises Hindu practice and thought

Two layers. The ṣaṇmata (six forms of worship) are for daily ritual: pick your iṣṭa-devatā, worship all six in the pañcāyatana. The ṣaḍdarśana (six Vedic schools) are for theology: how one reasons about the self, the world and Brahman. If you want to follow a path outside the Hindu fold, there are the three avaidika schools: Cārvāka, Jaina and Bauddha.

1 · Ṣaṇmata: six forms of worship

Tradition credits Śaṅkara as ṣaṇmata-sthāpanācārya. Smārta households (mainly in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Kerala and Maharashtra) worship all six in the pañcāyatana pūjā, with one chosen as the central deity.

2 · Ṣaḍdarśana: six Vedic schools

The Sarva-siddhānta-saṅgraha, traditionally credited to Śaṅkara (authorship doubted), ranks the schools as steps toward Advaita. The right-hand column gives Śaṅkara's position in his Brahmasūtra Bhāṣya.
