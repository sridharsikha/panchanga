Tithi — Lunar Day

Moon − Sun = angular separation ÷ 12°
—
How far has the Moon pulled ahead of the Sun? Every 12° of separation = 1 Tithi. This is what creates the waxing and waning — the Moon's phase you see in the sky.

Nakshatra — Lunar Mansion

Moon's position in the 27-star belt (each 13°20')
—
Where is the Moon against the 27 star-fields? Blue = Moon's Nakshatra (changes every ~1 day). Gold = Sun's Nakshatra (changes every ~13.5 days). Watch the Moon lap the Sun ~13 times in a year!

Yoga — Luni-Solar Day

Moon + Sun = combined longitude ÷ 13°20'
—
ADD the Moon's position and the Sun's position together. The combined energy of both luminaries. Think of it as: "where is the centre of gravity of the sky's light?" Divides into 27 Yogas.

Karana — Half Tithi

Same as Tithi but ÷ 6° (half the resolution)
—
Zoom into the Tithi. Cut each Tithi in half = 2 Karanas per Tithi, 60 per lunar month. It's the same Moon−Sun angle, just measured at 6° precision instead of 12°. Gives you quarter-day timing.

The Elegant Geometry

All four elements come from just two numbers: the Sun's longitude and the Moon's longitude as seen from Earth.

Tithi = Moon minus Sun → How far apart are they? This is the DIFFERENCE. It gives you the Moon's phase — waxing, full, waning, new.

Nakshatra = Moon alone → Where is the Moon in the sky? Sun is irrelevant. Pure position against the 27 star-fields. One mansion per day.

Yoga = Moon plus Sun → The SUM. Both luminaries' positions combined. Think of Tithi as "tension between Sun & Moon" and Yoga as "total light energy in the sky." Same 27 divisions as Nakshatra, but measuring something completely different.

Karana = Half of Tithi → Same formula as Tithi (Moon − Sun), just at double the resolution. 6° per Karana instead of 12° per Tithi. Like zooming in on your watch from hours to half-hours.

Together: subtraction (Tithi), position (Nakshatra), addition (Yoga), and half-subtraction (Karana) — they capture every possible angular relationship between Earth, Moon, and the stars. Nothing is left unmeasured.

The outer ring shows the 12 Rāshis (30° each). The inner ring shows 27 Nakshatras (13°20' each). Both divide the same 360° sky — each Rāshi contains exactly 2¼ Nakshatras. When the Sun crosses from one Rāshi to the next, that moment is a Sankramana. Makara Sankrānti = Sun entering Makara (Capricorn).
